How I decided a company was new

Every company that wins a federal contract registers with the government and gets a 12-character ID called a Unique Entity ID. I used that ID as the company’s identity.

First, I asked USAspending.gov for every contract USDA newly awarded between Oct. 13 and Nov. 12, 2026. That means the first action on the contract was in that window, not a change to an older contract. It includes purchase orders, definitive contracts and orders placed under existing contract vehicles.

Then, for each company’s ID, I searched for any USDA contract (including umbrella contracts that agencies order from) with any recorded activity from Oct. 13, 2021, through Oct. 12, 2026. If that search came back empty, I counted the company as new to USDA.

Finally, as a check, I searched those companies’ names to see whether a company with the same name but a different ID had USDA contracts in the same five years. Those are flagged below.

What this method could get wrong

  • One company can have more than one ID. Big firms often register divisions, branches or subsidiaries separately. That is why US Foods and Phoenix Software International show up here even though companies with their exact names have USDA contracts under other IDs. I flagged exact name matches, but a related company with a different name (a parent, a subsidiary or a renamed firm) would not be caught.
  • Joint ventures get their own IDs. A joint venture can be brand new on paper while its partners are longtime USDA contractors, as with Kenall-Halff JV below.
  • Only prime contracts count. A company could have worked for USDA as a subcontractor, or received USDA grants, loans or farm payments, and still appear new here.
  • Small purchases are often missing. Many government credit card buys and other small purchases are not reported to USAspending.gov, so a company with only small past sales to USDA may look new.
  • The data is still coming in. Agencies report contracts after the fact, so contracts from the last few days may be missing, and dollar amounts can change as contracts are modified. The amount shown is what USDA had obligated as of when I pulled the data, not the contract’s maximum possible value.
  • Agency labels come from USAspending.gov. Some USDA offices that buy on behalf of others are labeled “Office of the Chief Financial Officer.” The specific buying office is shown next to it.
  • The data can have errors. Misreported IDs, names or dates in the government’s records would carry through to this list. Check each contract’s page before relying on it.
